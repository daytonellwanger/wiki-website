# Specs

<!-- purpose
Any medium-to-large change requires a spec. A spec is a natural language description of the change you want to make, why you want to make it, how it will be made, and why you decided the things you decided. If an AI agent will be writing the code, a spec is its input.

See [template.md](template.md) for the spec format used in this repo.

## When to write a spec

Write a spec for anything that could be considered a design decision: a new feature, updating a public interface, changing the interaction between two components.

Bug fixes and small changes (renaming, small refactors, config tweaks) don't need a spec.

## What a spec covers

**Problem.** What is broken or missing, and why does it matter? This should stand alone — a reader with no prior context should understand the motivation.

**Scope and non-goals.** What is explicitly out of scope for this change. Prevents scope creep and documents intentional deferrals.

**Proposed solution.** What will be built or changed. Enough detail to reason about correctness and identify risks, but not a restatement of the code. Diagrams, data models, or API shapes should be included where they clarify the design.

**Alternatives considered.** (Optional) What other approaches were evaluated and why they were rejected. This is what prevents future engineers or AI from "improving" the design back to a rejected alternative.
